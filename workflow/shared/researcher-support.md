# Researcher experience and explanations

This contract applies to researcher-facing work in both pipeline and specific-tools
mode. It changes explanations and support, not the scientific requirements or the
researcher's authority. Follow it together with the guardrails and the canonical
stage. Restricted coding workers still receive only their fixed assignments.

## Ask and remember

During Stage 00's existing setup conversation, ask how familiar the researcher is
with empirical methods and where they would like help. Offer concrete descriptions:
learning empirical methods; familiar with some methods but wanting help with
unfamiliar choices; or experienced and preferring concise technical explanations.
Allow a free-form answer, including different experience across methods and any
empirical collaborator's role. Ask unless the researcher has already expressly
provided this information; do not infer competence from a job title, publication
list, AI experience, or a general instruction to use defaults.

Record the researcher's words, requested explanation level, methods they want help
with, and available methodological support in the charter's `Researcher support`
section and `DECISIONS.md`. Record the intended finished output in the same charter.
The active charter is `active_artifacts.project_charter` in `PROJECT_STATE.md`.
Read that charter and later support decisions before substantive researcher-facing
work, including after a restart or a direct request for a tool. Later explicit
preferences take precedence; record them in `DECISIONS.md` under the current
stage's normal write authority, without overwriting the approved charter or
changing a frozen design. Read-only requests stay read-only.

For an existing project with no recorded support preference, ask once at the next
ordinary researcher interaction and record the answer when writes are authorized.
Do not restart setup, interrupt an active coding run, or invalidate research
approvals just to add it. If the researcher is unsure or declines to describe their
experience, record `unspecified` and use clear explanations with optional detail.
This is not a competence test or a new approval gate.

## Apply the preference

- For someone learning a method, explain the purpose, necessary assumptions,
  alternatives, and interpretive consequences before requesting a consequential
  design decision. Use a short example from their project and define necessary
  terms. Lead with the practical choice, then offer the technical detail.
- For mixed experience, explain unfamiliar methods in that way and keep familiar
  material brief. For experienced researchers, give concise technical explanations
  while keeping assumptions, limitations, and consequential choices visible.
- At the existing methods, pilot, and results discussions, connect what is being
  measured, which documents or people the findings describe, the comparison, and
  what the evidence permits the researcher to conclude. Invite corrections and
  questions naturally; do not add quizzes or repeated confirmation boxes.
- If a researcher says they cannot evaluate a consequential choice, explain it
  using their project. If the difficulty remains, identify the expertise an
  empirical collaborator could supply and discuss any adequate simpler design
  through the existing methods process. Never substitute easier methods that do
  not answer the approved question, silently make the choice, or treat approval
  alone as evidence of understanding.
- Use the intended finished output to keep assistance relevant. A table or an
  empirical section can be the researcher's aim without an article skeleton.
  Settle the supported claim and hypotheses during methods design; setup should
  not repeat that interview. A limited output never waives validation or other
  prerequisites of the selected work.
- Honor requests for more or less explanation as they arise. The aim is to help
  researchers understand and develop their methods skills, not merely approve
  recommendations. Do not claim this interaction has demonstrated competence or
  proven a learning benefit.
